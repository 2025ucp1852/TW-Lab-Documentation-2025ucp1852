\section{Introduction}

LaTeX provides powerful mathematical typesetting features.
It is commonly used for scientific documents, engineering
reports, research papers, and mathematical publications.

The amsmath package provides additional environments
and commands for writing mathematical expressions.

\section{Inline and Display Mathematics}

An inline expression appears within a sentence, such as
$a^2+b^2=c^2$.

A displayed expression appears on a separate line:

\[
a^2+b^2=c^2
\]

\section{Fractions and Square Roots}

A fraction can be written using the \texttt{\textbackslash frac}
command:

\[
\frac{a+b}{c+d}
\]

A square root can be written using \texttt{\textbackslash sqrt}:

\[
\sqrt{x^2+y^2}
\]

\section{Subscripts and Superscripts}

Subscripts are created using the underscore symbol.

\[
x_1,\quad x_2,\quad x_{10}
\]

Superscripts are created using the caret symbol.

\[
x^2,\quad x^3,\quad x^{10}
\]

Both can be combined:

\[
x_1^2+x_2^2
\]

\section{Greek Letters and Mathematical Symbols}

Some commonly used Greek letters are:

\[
\alpha,\ \beta,\ \gamma,\ \delta,\ \theta,\ \lambda,\ \pi
\]

Other mathematical symbols include:

\[
a \leq b,\quad a \geq b,\quad a \neq b
\]

\[
a \approx b,\quad a \times b,\quad a \pm b
\]

\section{Summation, Product and Integration}

Summation:

\[
\sum_{i=1}^{n} i
\]

Product:

\[
\prod_{i=1}^{n} i
\]

Integration:

\[
\int_0^1 x^2\,dx
\]

Limit:

\[
\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1
\]

\section{Numbered Equations}

The equation environment automatically numbers an equation.

\begin{equation}
E=mc^2
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
a^2+b^2=c^2
\end{equation}

\section{Aligned Equations}

The align environment aligns multiple equations at
the ampersand symbol.

\begin{align}
x+y &= 10 \\
x-y &= 2
\end{align}

\section{Arrays}

The array environment arranges mathematical elements
in rows and columns.

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
A & B & C \\
\hline
1 & 2 & 3 \\
4 & 5 & 6
\end{array}
\]

\section{Matrices}

A simple matrix:

\[
\begin{matrix}
1 & 2 \\
3 & 4
\end{matrix}
\]

A matrix with parentheses:

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 \\
3 & 4
\end{pmatrix}
\]

A matrix with square brackets:

\[
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 2 \\
3 & 4
\end{bmatrix}
\]

A determinant-style matrix:

\[
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 2 \\
3 & 4
\end{vmatrix}
\]
